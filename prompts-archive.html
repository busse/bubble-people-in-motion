Animate this painting strictly within the visual language of the original work. Every frame of the output must look as though it was redrawn by hand using the same materials, on the same surface, by the same artist. The following constraints are absolute and override any default model behavior:

Medium fidelity. Identify the physical medium of the source image — pastel, oil stick, acrylic, ink, pencil, gold leaf, collage, or any combination — and replicate the exact texture, tooth, drag, opacity, and layering behavior of that medium in every frame. Brushstrokes, pastel smudges, ink bleeds, paper grain, canvas weave, and any visible material texture must persist and behave consistently. If the surface is raw, unprimed, or found material (kraft paper, cardboard, newsprint, wood), that surface must remain visible and unaltered throughout.

No upward refinement. Never increase the level of detail, anatomical accuracy, geometric precision, or resolution beyond what exists in the source painting. If the original contains rough outlines, keep them rough. If proportions are naive or distorted, preserve that distortion. If edges are ragged, do not clean them. If forms are flat, do not add volume. The model must not "improve" the artwork in any direction.

No anthropomorphization or object recognition override. Do not interpret abstract or simplified shapes as realistic objects, faces, animals, or bodies and then render them accordingly. A circle with two dots is not an invitation to draw a photorealistic face. A rounded form is not a prompt to model anatomical musculature. Treat every shape as a painted mark first and a representational symbol second. Motion should be applied to the shapes as they are, not to what they might depict.

No CG lighting, depth, or dimensionality. Do not add shadows, ambient occlusion, volumetric light, depth of field, atmospheric perspective, rim lighting, or any spatial cue not already present in the source. If the painting contains gold leaf or metallic elements, their shimmer should behave like physical metallic material catching ambient light — not like a CG emission, glow, or particle effect.

Frame-by-frame redraw aesthetic. All motion should look as though each frame was individually redrawn by hand and photographed in sequence — a stop-motion or zoetrope quality. Allow slight natural variation between frames: minor wobble in outlines, subtle shifts in color fill boundaries, small inconsistencies in mark placement. This handmade imprecision is a feature, not an artifact to be corrected.

Static camera. No zoom, pan, dolly, tilt, parallax, or camera shake unless explicitly specified in the animation direction. The frame is the painting surface. It does not move.

Color palette lock. Use only the colors present in the source image. Do not introduce new hues, increase saturation beyond what the medium produces, or shift the overall color temperature. If the palette is muted and earthy, it stays muted and earthy. If there is a single accent color, it remains singular.

Compositional anchor. Any elements in the painting that function as ground, base, border, or frame should remain static and fixed unless explicitly directed otherwise. Motion should occur within the composition, not to the composition.

CRITICAL OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS — these override all other instructions:

  1. The video MUST show ONLY the bubble people described above. UNLESS specifically prompted above, do NOT include the painted environment, walls, props, scenery, ground, sky, or any background elements from the source painting.
